Ricci flow on a dumbbell: the neck pinches, then one surgery

The page computes the flow itself when it loads. A 3-manifold whose cross-sections are round 2-spheres of radius ψ, with length element φ dx, follows ψt = ψss − (1 − ψs2)/ψ and φt = 2(ψss/ψ)φ, with d/ds = (1/φ)d/dx (1601 grid points, explicit steps; the two ends are bulb equators with reflecting boundaries and are closed with round caps in the drawing; each 2-sphere is drawn as a circle).

The frames are spaced evenly in log h, where h is the neck radius, from 0.26 down to 0.01. The zoom (bottom left) always shows ±15h along the axis and ±4h across it, so it shows the neck's shape: it gets straighter as it thins, but slowly. The numbers on the right are the curvature of the neck, the largest slope |ψs| within ±4h, and the length of the part thinner than 2h.

At h = 0.01 the neck is cut at its thinnest cross-section and each side gets a small cap of radius h. The cap is drawn as a hemisphere; Perelman glues a fixed standard cap, whose exact shape is not reproduced here. The pieces are drawn slightly apart. Each piece is a large bulb carrying a thin horn. The drawing stops there: the later flow of the pieces is not computed.
